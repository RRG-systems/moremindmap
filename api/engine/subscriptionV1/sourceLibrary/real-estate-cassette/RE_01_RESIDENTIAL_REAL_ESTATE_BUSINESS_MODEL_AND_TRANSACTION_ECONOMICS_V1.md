## 1. Mission

Model the residential agent/team transaction engine from opportunity through owner profit, including listing/buyer mix, brokerage economics and fallout.

## 2. Scope

- units, volume and average sale price
- GCI, split, cap, royalty/franchise/transaction fees
- listing versus buyer economics and time use
- fallout, seasonality and pipeline lag
- solo versus team contribution and owner profit

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Volume and GCI can rise while agent net or owner profit falls.
- Listing inventory can create leverage and secondary demand; buyer work often consumes more variable time.
- Pipeline and seasonality separate current activity from current income.
- Brokerage/team splits change incentives, capacity and net economics.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- closed/pending/failed transactions by side/source
- commission statements and brokerage/team agreements
- time/cost by listing and buyer side
- pipeline age and fallout reasons
- owner/team P&L excluding leader production

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- What produces GCI and what remains after every split/fee/expense?
- Which side/source produces profit and strategic leverage?
- How much production depends on current pipeline versus old activity?
- What is team profit without leader production?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- GCI-to-net bridge is explicit.
- Listing/buyer mix matches capacity and strategy.
- Fallout and seasonality are modeled.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- GCI is treated as income/profit.
- Brokerage cap is treated as total cost.
- Listing/buyer sides are assumed economically identical.
- Team volume hides leader subsidy.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- A buyer-heavy model can be highly profitable with systems/leverage.
- A listing can be strategically valuable despite lower immediate margin.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
