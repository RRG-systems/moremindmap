## 1. Mission

Model the durability and economic value of customer relationships after acquisition, including retention, repeat, expansion, advocacy and referral.

## 2. Scope

- retention/churn
- repeat and renewal
- cohort lifetime value
- customer health
- expansion/contraction
- referral/advocacy value
- service recovery
- relationship continuity
- second-order value

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Retention compounds lifetime contribution and reduces replacement demand.
- Acquisition source can predict fit, retention and referral behavior.
- Delivery quality and expectation fit influence continuity and advocacy.
- Referral value includes direct customer value and second-order introductions.
- High gross retention can hide contraction or low contribution.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- cohort retention/repeat/renewal
- contribution over lifetime
- expansion/contraction
- complaints/recovery and outcome quality
- referral source and downstream value
- customer health/usage
- reason-coded churn/non-repeat
- segment/source differences

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- Which customers stay/repeat/refer and why?
- What is observed lifetime contribution by cohort/source?
- Where does promise-delivery mismatch create churn?
- Is referral dependence durable or key-person concentrated?
- What recovery action changes retention?
- Are retained customers economically healthy?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Cohort/source/segment differences are visible.
- Retention is tied to delivered outcome and fit.
- Referral is tracked as a relationship chain, not folklore.
- Recovery learning changes operations.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Blended retention/LTV hides weak cohorts.
- Revenue retention ignores contribution.
- Referral claims are not source-attributed.
- Acquisition masks churn.
- Customer success activity is not linked to outcomes.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- Low repeat may be inherent in infrequent categories.
- High churn may reflect intentional low-fit filtering.
- Referral can occur long after service and be under-attributed.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
