## 1. Mission

Determine whether the business creates economic value, converts that value into cash, and can fund its operating and growth obligations.

## 2. Scope

- revenue quality
- gross and contribution margin
- fully loaded acquisition and delivery cost
- unit economics
- cash conversion and working capital
- fixed/variable cost structure
- capital intensity
- cohort/customer economics
- break-even and sensitivity

## 3. Non-Scope

- Current market, law, regulation, rate, platform, competitor and benchmark facts belong to Dynamic Research.
- Outcome claims about what intervention works for whom belong to future RSL after execution/outcome evidence.
- Whole-Person Authority may explain implementation friction or fit; it cannot establish business facts.
- Customer report choreography, required wording, Five Futures runtime math and production integration are outside this Bible.

## 4. Core Primitives

- revenue
- gross margin
- contribution margin

- payback period
- cash conversion cycle
- working capital
- fixed cost

- operating leverage
- runway
- break-even

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Growth multiplies unit economics; negative contribution accelerates loss.
- Profit can coexist with cash stress when collections lag obligations.
- Blended averages hide unprofitable segments, cohorts and channels.
- High fixed cost increases upside after break-even and downside below it.
- Long payback plus churn or weak repeat creates an acquisition treadmill.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- income statements and cash records
- revenue/cost by product, segment, cohort and channel
- collections/payment timing
- customer retention/repeat data
- sales and delivery labor allocation
- capital expenditures/debt obligations
- owner compensation and unpriced owner labor

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- What remains after all truly variable costs?
- What is fully loaded CAC and payback by source?
- Where does accounting profit diverge from cash?
- Which customer/product/channel destroys value?
- What cost or capital arrives before revenue?
- What assumption drives the break-even conclusion?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Cohort/segment economics are visible.
- Cash and accrual views are reconciled.
- Owner labor and shared costs are not hidden.
- Growth is funded by positive economics or explicit capital strategy.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Revenue used as a proxy for health.
- GCI/gross billings confused with owner profit.
- CAC excludes sales labor or failed demand.
- LTV is projected without observed retention.
- One-period snapshot ignores seasonality and cash timing.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- High CAC may be rational with high verified contribution and retention.
- Low current margin can be rational during bounded learning/investment.
- Negative cash flow can be planned when financing and milestones are explicit.

## 12. Business-Model Differences

- SaaS emphasizes recurring gross margin, retention and payback.
- Services emphasizes utilization, realization, scope and senior labor.
- Transactional models emphasize volume, take rate, fallout and pipeline timing.
- Local services emphasize route density, labor/material efficiency and repeat.
- Capital-intensive models emphasize utilization, debt service and cash conversion.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
