## 1. Mission

Determine whether the offer solves a valued problem for a defined customer, is differentiated from alternatives, and supports pricing and margin power.

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Relative value versus credible alternatives drives conversion and price realization.
- Weak proof raises perceived risk and price sensitivity.
- An offer can convert but destroy margin or delivery capacity.
- Discounting can mask unclear value while training the market to wait.
- Positioning determines the comparison set and therefore willingness to pay.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- win/loss and objection reasons
- segment-specific conversion
- price/discount history
- competitive alternatives
- customer interviews and usage/outcomes
- margin/cost-to-serve by package
- renewal/repeat/referral behavior
- promise-delivery gap

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- For whom is the offer most valuable and why now?
- What alternative is the customer comparing?
- What proof reduces risk?
- Is low conversion an offer, positioning, demand-quality, process or capacity issue?
- Does the price support acquisition and delivery economics?
- Which features/outcomes are customers actually paying for?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Clear segment/problem/outcome/proof.
- Pricing reflects value and economics rather than fear.
- Packages align cost-to-serve and customer need.
- Differentiation survives competitor comparison.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Features substitute for outcome.
- Everyone is the target customer.
- Price is cut before diagnosis.
- Marketing message promises what operations cannot deliver.
- A custom offer expands scope without price or capacity control.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- Low conversion can be caused by poor lead quality or sales execution.
- Premium positioning may intentionally lower volume.
- Regulated or commodity markets may constrain price discretion.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
