## 1. Mission

Determine how the business creates value for a defined customer, delivers that value, captures economic value, and reproduces the system without hidden structural contradiction.

## 2. Scope

- customer and job-to-be-done
- value proposition and value chain
- revenue and transaction architecture
- recurring versus transactional logic
- asset, labor, capital and channel intensity
- value capture and transferability
- model alignment across demand, delivery, margin and organization

## 3. Non-Scope

- Current market, law, regulation, rate, platform, competitor and benchmark facts belong to Dynamic Research.
- Outcome claims about what intervention works for whom belong to future RSL after execution/outcome evidence.
- Whole-Person Authority may explain implementation friction or fit; it cannot establish business facts.
- Customer report choreography, required wording, Five Futures runtime math and production integration are outside this Bible.

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- A valuable offer can still fail when acquisition or delivery cost exceeds captured value.
- Recurring cost attached to episodic revenue creates timing and liquidity exposure.
- Founder-dependent creation/delivery can produce income without creating transferable enterprise capability.
- Every model embeds a required demand system, delivery system, capital pattern and organizational form.
- Local subsystem strength cannot rescue an incoherent model; misalignment migrates pressure into cash, capacity, quality or retention.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- customer segments and paid outcomes
- offer/revenue streams and terms
- gross/contribution margin by product/segment
- acquisition and delivery process
- renewal/repeat/transaction frequency
- key-person and channel dependence
- capital and working-capital requirements
- ownership of customer relationship and data

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- Who receives what valuable outcome, through which mechanism?
- Where is value captured versus leaked?
- What must be true operationally and economically for the model to work?
- Which subsystem is locally healthy but globally incompatible?
- Can value be reproduced without the founder or one channel?
- What event would invalidate the model rather than merely a tactic?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Clear segment/job/offer/economic loop.
- Demand and delivery economics are mutually supportable.
- Revenue timing and cost timing are explicitly understood.
- The company owns durable assets, relationships, systems or data that make reproduction easier.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Revenue masquerades as value creation.
- A high-cost demand engine feeds a low-margin offer.
- Growth goals assume capacity or capital the model cannot supply.
- One exceptional person hides an untransferable operating model.
- A transactional business carries recurring infrastructure without enough frequency.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- Early-stage experimentation can look incoherent while search is rational.
- Deliberate premium craftsmanship may be founder-intensive and economically sound.
- A loss-leading component can be rational when cohort/customer economics prove the system-level return.

## 12. Business-Model Differences

- Transactional: volatility, pipeline timing and repeat/referral matter.
- Recurring: retention, renewal, payback and service obligation dominate.
- Professional services: utilization, senior-talent constraint and scope control matter.
- SaaS: acquisition payback, retention, expansion and support/onboarding matter.
- Marketplace: liquidity, take rate, two-sided quality and trust matter.
- Asset/capital intensive: capacity, utilization, financing and cash conversion dominate.

## 15. Whole-Person Interaction Boundary

Use the frozen Whole-Person Model selectively to understand adoption, attention, decision, communication, pressure, leadership, role or implementation mechanics. Never convert a trait into a business cause without business evidence. Never override financial, operational, market or customer reality. Preserve person, business and relationship provenance separately.

## 16. Team and Organization Boundary

Retrieve only relevant people by business/team membership, role, reporting relationship, permission and timestamp. Test structure, incentives, workload, role design, authority, capacity and missing systems before personality. Never average people into a team personality.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.

## 19. Intervention Relevance

- narrow or change segment/job
- restructure offer or revenue terms
- change acquisition/delivery architecture
- remove structural cost mismatch
- build transferability and owned assets
- prove or disprove a model assumption through bounded evidence

This Bible may identify mechanisms, candidate movers, observability, reversibility, adoption burdens and evidence needed. It does not prescribe customer wording, claim causal effect, or determine One Move alone.
