## 1. Mission

Determine whether work is delivered repeatably at required quality and speed, and how process, variation, capacity and rework shape throughput and customer outcomes.

## 2. Scope

- process and handoffs
- standard work and exception handling
- quality and rework
- capacity/utilization/queues
- scheduling and work in process
- knowledge-work load
- tools/automation
- service-level and delivery risk

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Variation and rework consume capacity twice and destabilize flow.
- High utilization near the constraint increases queues and lead time.
- Automation multiplies both good and bad process.
- Knowledge-work capacity depends on skill, decision complexity, switching and review load, not hours alone.
- Unclear handoffs create invisible ownership and defects.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- process maps and stage timestamps
- volume, WIP, cycle/lead time
- capacity by role/skill
- defects/rework/exceptions
- quality/outcome/customer signals
- schedule adherence and interruptions
- tool/system usage
- service-level misses

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- Where does work wait, fail or return?
- What volume/complexity can the system handle at required quality?
- Which work requires scarce judgment?
- What is standardized versus exception-driven?
- Does automation remove or hide a bad handoff?
- What breaks first when demand doubles?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Flow, ownership and exceptions are visible.
- Standards protect quality while allowing bounded judgment.
- Constraint capacity and rework are measured.
- Systems reduce memory/heroic dependence.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Busyness is treated as utilization.
- Standardization becomes bureaucracy without value.
- Tools are added before process clarity.
- The founder catches every exception.
- Quality is measured only after customer harm.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- Slack may be resilience rather than waste.
- High variation can be intrinsic to expert/custom work.
- Some exceptions create strategic learning and should not be eliminated.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
