## 1. Mission

Determine whether decisions and commitments turn into faithful execution, feedback and learning through clear ownership, consequences and incentives.

## 2. Scope

- outcome ownership
- decision rights
- commitments and standards
- leading indicators and inspection
- incentives/consequences
- execution fidelity
- feedback cadence
- strategy-versus-execution discrimination

## 3. Non-Scope

- Current market, law, regulation, rate, platform, competitor and benchmark facts belong to Dynamic Research.
- Outcome claims about what intervention works for whom belong to future RSL after execution/outcome evidence.
- Whole-Person Authority may explain implementation friction or fit; it cannot establish business facts.
- Customer report choreography, required wording, Five Futures runtime math and production integration are outside this Bible.

## 4. Core Primitives

- learning loop

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Accountability requires clear ownership, observable standard, timely feedback and meaningful consequence.
- Diffuse ownership creates coordination failure and plausible deniability.
- Misaligned incentives create rational behavior that looks like low motivation.
- Low execution fidelity makes strategy evaluation invalid.
- Excessive control can suppress truthful reporting and adaptation.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- named owners/decision rights
- commitments and due dates
- leading/lagging measures
- review cadence and action logs
- adherence/fidelity
- incentive and consequence structures
- exceptions/escalations
- strategy changes and reasons

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- Who owns the outcome and can make the decision?
- What observable behavior/result defines fidelity?
- What happens when a commitment is missed?
- Are incentives aligned with system outcomes?
- Is the strategy weak or was it not executed?
- Does review create learning or theater?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- One accountable owner with required authority.
- Few meaningful measures and timely review.
- Misses produce diagnosis and correction.
- Incentives do not reward local harm.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Everyone owns it, so no one owns it.
- Activity is inspected without causal linkage.
- Fear creates green dashboards and hidden problems.
- Rewards optimize local volume at expense of margin/quality.
- Strategy churn follows low-fidelity execution.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- External shocks can defeat faithful execution.
- Novel work may require exploratory standards.
- Cultural/legal norms change acceptable consequence and feedback practices.

Retrieve only relevant people by business/team membership, role, reporting relationship, permission and timestamp. Test structure, incentives, workload, role design, authority, capacity and missing systems before personality. Never average people into a team personality.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.

## 19. Intervention Relevance

- clarify owner and decision right
- define fidelity and leading indicator
- align incentives
- shorten feedback loop
- separate strategy test from execution test
- change consequence/escalation
- reduce priority overload

This Bible may identify mechanisms, candidate movers, observability, reversibility, adoption burdens and evidence needed. It does not prescribe customer wording, claim causal effect, or determine One Move alone.
