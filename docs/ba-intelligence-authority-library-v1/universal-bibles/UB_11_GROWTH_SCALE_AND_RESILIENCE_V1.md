## 1. Mission

Explain what changes as volume, complexity and scope increase, and whether the business can absorb shocks without losing economic or operating integrity.

## 2. Scope

- scale economies/diseconomies
- complexity and coordination
- cash/capital requirements
- concentration and dependency
- quality dilution
- resilience/redundancy
- seasonality/shocks
- succession/continuity

## 5. Causal Mechanisms

- Growth multiplies positive or negative unit economics.
- Complexity and coordination can grow faster than productive capacity.
- Concentration converts channel/customer/key-person failure into cash risk.
- Scaling volume can dilute quality and retention before financial results reveal it.
- Resilience requires deliberate slack/options, which can look inefficient in stable periods.

## 6. Evidence Inputs

- growth by segment/channel/product
- unit economics and cash needs at scale
- capacity/quality/rework trends
- customer/channel/vendor/key-person concentration
- scenario and contingency plans
- seasonality and volatility
- capital access and covenants
- succession/continuity evidence

Evidence must retain source identity, date/period, units where relevant, provenance class, confidence, contradictions and missingness. Customer-stated evidence is not automatically verified; model inference is not observation.

## 7. Diagnostic Questions

- What changes nonlinearly when volume doubles?
- Which single dependency could materially impair cash or delivery?
- What growth consumes cash before creating it?
- Where are diseconomies already visible?
- Which resilience cost is deliberate versus accidental?
- Can relationships, systems and authority transfer?

## 8. Strong Patterns

- Growth is paced to economics and constraint.
- Concentration is measured and intentional.
- Quality/capacity leading indicators precede expansion.
- Contingencies protect critical dependencies.

Strong patterns are diagnostic hypotheses supported by evidence, not templates that force every business into the same conclusion.

## 9. Failure Modes

- Growth assumed to fix margin.
- One channel/customer/person is called a strategy without contingency.
- Headcount and tools precede model proof.
- Geographic/product expansion multiplies complexity.
- Short-term efficiency removes all slack.

## 10. Counterexamples and Confounds

- Focused concentration can create durable advantage.
- Temporary redundancy can support transition.
- Early-stage exploration is naturally volatile.

## 17. Evidence, Confidence and Uncertainty

- `OBSERVED`: directly supported by governed current evidence.
- `CUSTOMER_STATED`: attributed to the customer, not independently verified.
- `DERIVED`: formula or deterministic transformation with inputs/units/formula present.
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`: mechanism fits evidence but alternatives remain.
- `FOUNDER_HEURISTIC`: useful operating doctrine, qualified and parameterized.
- `DYNAMIC_CURRENT`: externally sourced current fact with retrieval date.
- `UNKNOWN`: missing, stale, contradictory or not safely inferable.

Confidence must be claim-specific. Missing or conflicting evidence survives into state; it is never replaced by a target, benchmark, generic profile value or provider prose.

## 18. Falsifiers

- The hypothesized mechanism is weakened when the named outcome changes while its alleged driver remains unchanged under comparable conditions.
- A competing mechanism explains the same evidence better and survives its own confound tests.
- Source lineage, period, units or identity do not support the claim.
- High execution fidelity produces no movement in the named leading indicators over the stated horizon, absent material confounds.
- New verified evidence contradicts the state contribution.
